Family Travel

Travelling with Grandparents and Little Ones

Family Travel·For three-generation trips

A hill trip with your parents and your children sounds simple until you start planning it. Will there be stairs to the bedroom? A sitting toilet? A rough road at the end? A doctor nearby if someone feels unwell? These are the questions families ask us most, so here are the honest answers, plus a gentle plan for the hills and the coast that nobody has to struggle through.

Why the villa works for three generations

The whole villa is yours
  • Ground-floor bedrooms: both bedrooms and their attached bathrooms are on the ground floor, with no stairs to climb. Only the loft is upstairs, and you won't need it for a small family.
  • European toilets: every bathroom has a sitting (European-style) toilet, with hot water from geysers.
  • Car right to the house: the road is tarred all the way to the villa, and you park right at the house. There are 2 small steps from the parking area to the front door, so it isn't fully step-free, but our caretaker will help with luggage.
  • A full kitchen: gas stove, fridge, utensils and crockery, so you can boil milk, make tea and cook light meals for the little ones and the elders, on your own schedule.
  • Fenced garden and terrace: the garden and the valley-facing terrace are fenced, and there's no pool. Bedroom 1 has a crib.
  • Quiet and private: no other guests, quiet hours at night, and power backup for lights, fans and Wi-Fi.
  • A caretaker on call: he lives close by and is on call throughout your stay.

Hospitals, milk and groceries nearby

24-hour hospital 1.4 km away

Peace of mind matters when you travel with elders and small children. Janathaa Hospital (KMCT Medical Centre) in Puduppadi is about 1.4 km away and open 24 hours. Mother Care Hospital, a private hospital on the highway at Engapuzha, is about 2 km away and there are bigger hospitals in Thamarassery town, about 6 km away.

Milk and daily groceries are at the shops near Perumpally Juma Masjid, less than 1 km from the villa, and the caretaker is happy to pick things up for you. Food isn't included, but several local restaurants, including vegetarian ones, deliver to the villa with their own delivery staff. Menus are kept at the house.

The drive from Mysuru and Bangalore

Mysuru: ~185 km · 4½–5 hrs

From Mysuru, the route runs via Gundlupet, Bandipur, Sulthan Bathery, Kalpetta and down the Thamarassery ghat on NH 766. It's a good national highway the whole way, with no off-road or estate driving. A few tips for a comfortable drive with elders and children:

  • Mind the Bandipur closure: the forest stretch is closed to traffic from 9 PM to 6 AM, so leave Mysuru by early afternoon at the latest.
  • Break the journey: stop at Gundlupet before the forest, or at Sulthan Bathery, for food, tea and a toilet break.
  • Take the ghat slowly: the Thamarassery ghat has nine hairpin bends. Drive gently, and keep motion-sickness tablets handy for anyone who needs them.
  • Arrive in daylight: check-in is from 2 PM, and arriving before dark makes the first evening easy.

Coming from Bangalore? The route is the same after Mysuru. See our Bangalore weekend route guide.

Gentle outings with little walking

Easy on the knees
  • Churam viewpoint: the ghat's famous view, which you can enjoy from beside the car.
  • Pookode Lake: near Lakkidi at the top of the ghat, with a flat path around the lake and pedal boats for the children.
  • Kozhikode Beach: about 55 minutes away. Walk the flat promenade at sunset, then have lunch or dinner at Paragon. See our beach guide for the other beaches.
  • The terrace at home: honestly, many of our guests' favourite part. Tea on the terrace while the mist rolls over the valley needs no planning at all.

Better to skip with elders: steep climbs like Chembra Peak, the long walk and steps up to Edakkal Caves, and the upper falls at Thusharagiri. Younger members of the group can do these while the grandparents rest at the villa.

A relaxed 2-night plan

No rushing

Day 1: Leave Mysuru by late morning, stop for lunch at Gundlupet or Sulthan Bathery, and reach the villa in the afternoon. Tea on the terrace, an early dinner delivered to the door. Day 2: A slow morning, then a drive up the ghat to Pookode Lake, or down to Kozhikode Beach for an evening by the sea. Back to the villa for a quiet night. Day 3: Breakfast with the view, check out by 11 AM and head home through Bandipur in daylight.
