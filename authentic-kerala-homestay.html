What Makes a Kerala Homestay Authentic

Travel Guide·From Naz's Farm House

"Homestay" gets used loosely in Kerala — some are family-run houses that have opened their doors, others are small resorts borrowing the word because it sounds warmer. If you're planning a trip and searching for an authentic Kerala homestay rather than a hotel with a nicer name, here's what actually separates the two, and why an eco stay off the beaten path near Wayanad tends to give you a different trip entirely.

It's Someone's Actual Home

Owner-run

Naz's Farm House is exactly that — a house Shanavas built for his own family in the hills above Kozhikode, that guests now get to stay in too. There's no reception desk, no shift-change of staff who don't know the property; it's the same person who built the garden telling you where the good sunset spot is. That's the difference an authentic Kerala homestay is actually about — not a design aesthetic, but a real household you're a guest in.

An Eco Stay By How It's Lived In, Not By a Certificate

Garden, not grounds

We're not chasing a green-certification logo — this is an eco stay in the plainer sense: a self-catered kitchen instead of a room-service menu, a garden you can actually walk through instead of manicured grounds you're kept off, and days shaped by the hills and weather rather than a printed activity schedule. If low-impact, close-to-nature travel matters to you, this is what it tends to look like on the ground.

Off the Beaten Path, On Purpose

Puduppadi, Thamarassery

We're not on a strip of stays competing for the same view — Naz's Farm House sits on 8 Acre Road in Puduppadi, on the Kozhikode side of the ghat road climbing into Wayanad, closer to a working village than a tourist cluster. It's an easy drive from Kozhikode and a short one from the start of the ghat, but it's not a place you'd stumble onto without meaning to — which is exactly the point for travelers trying to get past the postcard version of Kerala.

What's Different From a Resort Stay

Whole property, one group

A booking here is the whole property — both bedrooms, the double-height hall, and The Majlis loft — not a room among strangers on a shared buffet schedule. There's no check-in queue or resort itinerary; check-in and check-out are worked out directly with us over WhatsApp, around your actual travel plans. It's a quieter, more independent way to stay, and it won't suit everyone — if you want a pool, a spa menu, and daily housekeeping rounds, a resort will serve you better.

Who This Kind of Stay Actually Suits

Independent travelers

Honestly: people who'd rather cook a meal in a real kitchen than order off a laminated menu, who want a couple of days of genuine quiet over a full activity roster, and who are fine asking the host directly for the waterfall or the good breakfast spot rather than reading it off a hotel binder. If that's the trip you're after, an authentic Kerala homestay like this is worth seeking out over the bigger, more polished options.
